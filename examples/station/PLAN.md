# The Drowned Station: plan

An adventure game built with mciwb: a chain of underwater glass domes, each
with a puzzle or a battle, joined by glass tunnels that pump dry as the
players restore the station. Steampunk machinery, a final boss, one to many
players. Inspired by an earlier hand-built map, "The Adventure of Doom" (2020),
whose best ideas it keeps (see "From the original").

Opening title: "The Adventure of Doom II: The Drowned Station".

This file is the agreed design and the order of work. Keep it current: when a
decision changes, change it here. Progress goes in `PROGRESS.md` beside it.

## Decisions (agreed with the owner)

| Topic | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Audience | A younger player and an adult, friends welcome. About an hour a run. Puzzles take some thought but never stall a run; fights are a fair challenge on Easy, not punishing. |
| Site | The natural deep ocean nearest the world's spawn, so sea life passes the glass. |
| Domes | Mixed sizes: about 32 across for small puzzle domes, 48 for most, 64 for the boss (its floor dug into the seabed). Some domes may break the surface and show air at the top. |
| Start | Spawn on the shore. Row a boat to a dock offshore. A lift goes down from the dock to the start dome. |
| Shape | A ring of eight domes: the start dome, six domes and the boss dome, whose exit tunnel leads back into the start dome. |
| Logic | A datapack of functions, generated by the build and copied into the world's `datapacks` folder, reloaded over RCON. Blocks and redstone are placed by mciwb. |
| Two players | Counted live, per dome: a dome with one player in it is in its solo form, with two or more in its two-player form, so a player elsewhere never strands the one in the room. Never more than two people needed. If a player leaves, it falls back to solo within a few seconds. |
| Death | Keep-inventory on. Respawn at the entrance of the last dome opened. Kit handed out by the game, not chests. Food never runs out. No golden apple hoard. |
| Reset | Buttons only in the start dome. First press starts a 60 s countdown on a bossbar ("Reset: 1 of 2, 57 s"); when every player in the game has pressed, everything goes back to the start, kits are cleared and players are put on the shore. A player's own presses count once. One player alone must press twice: the first press starts the countdown ("Reset: press again to confirm, 57 s"), a second within it resets (owner, after the first full play-through: one press was too easy to do by accident). Creative and spectator players are not counted. Each dome entrance has a "recall" button: teleport to the start dome, no progress lost. |
| Difficulty | A level from 1 to 10, default 5 (5 is the level the game is tuned for). Chosen on a dial beside the map wall in the start dome, by the same vote as the reset: a player sets the dial to a proposed level and presses confirm, a 60 s countdown shows on a bossbar ("Difficulty 7: 1 of 2"), and it takes effect when every player in the game has confirmed; one player alone presses confirm twice, as for the reset. It scales what can be scaled without rebuilding: mob numbers, health and damage, the boss's health, the length of the Signal Room's sequence, timers and the maze's clock. A new level applies from the next dome entered, not to a fight in progress. The level survives a reset. The best time is kept per level, and the trophy sign records the level. |
| Site independence | Every position in the layout is an offset from one anchor (a point and a floor height), and an offline check tests a layout against a survey file, so re-siting in another world is a new survey and a new anchor. |
| Story | An old undersea pumping station has gone dark and its master engine has turned on its makers. The players are the repair crew. Told briefly: a title and a sound on entering and on solving a dome, a brass plaque per dome, a short logbook by the last engineer in each. |
| Scoreboard | In the start dome: a wall-sized brass map with a lamp per dome and a glowing tunnel between them; the run's time and the best time; an on-screen line that follows players ("Domes 3 of 7, 12:40"). Winners get a trophy sign for a Wall of Fame, which the reset leaves alone: at the win a dispenser by the Wall lights up ("YOUR TROPHY") and a title says so; each player in the game at the win presses its button once for their own sign, whose lore says where it goes. |
| Tunnel | Present from the start, flooded and dark behind a brass airlock. The airlock has a glass window so the flooded tunnel can be seen from the dome. On success the pump engine by the airlock wakes (its lamps lit, a hum, a lever by the door's window; "Pull the pump lever"); any player's pull of that lever runs it (pistons, steam, a klaxon), the water level drops layer by layer while the players watch through the window, lamps come on towards the next dome, the airlock opens. The same event after every dome, the Engine's tunnel home at the win included; the first tunnel's lever is the Pump House's master lever. |
| Boss | A machine with phases, in the 64 dome. Phase 1: shut three steam valves round the dome while automatons pour out (two players: two valves thrown together). Phase 2: the engine cracks open and its master comes out, a brass-armoured brute on a ravager (a nod to the original). Health scales with the number of players. The dead respawn in an antechamber and walk back in. |
| Code | A self-contained mciwb example, `examples/station/`, on branch `examples/station`: layout, a module per dome, the datapack generator, a verify script, a guide, a unit test of the offline checks. It goes to main by pull request. |
| Staging | A playable slice first, then the owner plays each stage before the next is built (see "Stages"). |

## The domes

Ring order. Each dome: one clear idea, a plaque, a logbook, a recall button
at the entrance, one real working machine to watch, and its tunnel pump.

0. **Start dome (the Pump House).** The lift from the dock arrives here. A
   master lever starts the run's clock and pumps the first tunnel dry, so the
   pump event is seen before any puzzle. The difficulty dial. The
   map wall, the timers, the briefing (a plaque and a short book), the reset
   buttons, the Wall of Fame, and the sealed door the boss tunnel returns by.
1. **Boiler Room** (tutorial). Feed the furnaces and turn valves to raise
   steam; gauges of lamps show pressure. *Two players:* two valves far apart
   must be held at once.
2. **Gearworks** (building). Lay copper "pipe" that only sticks to a brass
   track (`can_place_on`) to link an engine to the door, with a tool that
   breaks only that pipe (`can_break`). Laid pipe is kept when the dome is
   left; only the game's reset clears it.
3. **Flooded Dome.** Half under water. Find and work the pumps to drain it
   level by level; each level uncovers the next pump. *Two players:* one
   holds a pump lever open while the other swims through.
4. **Foundry** (battle). Waves of clockwork automatons while a machine
   charges; survive until it is charged. Mob numbers scale with players.
5. **Signal Room.** Repeat a sequence of lamps and notes on a big brass
   console; the sequence is different each run. *Two players:* the console
   splits into two halves that take turns.
6. **Clockwork Maze.** A maze whose walls shift on a visible clock, with
   piston lifts between levels.
7. **Boss dome (the Engine).** As above.

## From the original

Keep:
- Restricted items as a puzzle (Gearworks): adventure mode becomes building.
- The Wall of Fame: a sign placeable only on the trophy wall, the winner
  writes their own line.
- Seeing where you have been: lit domes stay visible through the glass.
- Rooms that put themselves right when entered.
- One joke: a "teleport uncalibrated" plate somewhere that sends you
  somewhere useless and back.

Avoid:
- A boss that can be outlasted (no hoard of healing; health scales).
- Commands aimed at the nearest player or at everyone, which misfire with
  several players. Target by position (who is in this dome) and by tag.
- Respawning beside the boss.
- Rewards with no use.

## Defaults chosen by the planner (not asked; change if wrong)

- **Look.** Waxed copper in mixed oxidation, cut copper, copper grates,
  doors, trapdoors and bulbs, lightning rods and chains (`iron_chain` and
  `copper_chain`: 26.3 has no plain `chain`) as pipework, dark oak, deepslate and polished blackstone floors, iron bars, gold blocks as
  "brass" for anything the player must notice. Tinted and plain glass for
  domes; sea lanterns and copper bulbs for light. Check every block and item
  name against the server's version before using it.
- **Rooms are busy, not bare** (owner, after the first play-test). Each dome
  has levels: walkways, gantries and platforms with stairs or ladders,
  pipe runs, tanks and boilers, and "mysterious" redstone machinery that
  ticks away on its own (clocks, pistons, lamps chasing, copper bulbs
  blinking, bells), decorative and safe to ignore. It must never be mistaken
  for a puzzle input, never block the way, and must survive a reset. The
  map wall reads as a map at a glance: a title, a frame, a "you are here"
  marker, a legend, the sea and the shore drawn in.
- **The lift is slow** enough to savour: about ten seconds a trip.
- **Real redstone.** Puzzle inputs are real levers, buttons, plates, hoppers
  and targets, and each dome has one working machine built in visible
  redstone. The datapack reads their states and drives what redstone cannot
  (progress, tunnels, mobs, timers, reset). Do not hide logic in redstone
  that the datapack could do more reliably.
- **Game rules** (this world is only for the game; 26.3 names them in
  snake_case): keep-inventory on (`keep_inventory`), mob griefing off
  (`mob_griefing`), natural monster spawning off (`spawn_monsters`, not
  `spawn_mobs`: the game summons its own; fish and squid stay), fire spread
  off (`fire_spread_radius_around_player`, a radius in blocks, 128 by
  default, not on/off: 0 turns it off), block drops off (`block_drops`:
  a block broken by a player or a function leaves no item).
  Leave the day cycle and weather running (`advance_time`,
  `advance_weather`). Difficulty stays Easy; the fights must
  check the difficulty is above Peaceful.
- **Players in the game** are those in adventure mode inside the station's
  box (dock to domes). The on-screen line and bossbars go only to them.
- **Kit.** Start: iron armour, sword, bow, arrows. Before the Foundry: a
  shield and better arrows. Before the boss: a brass-themed upgrade. Given by
  function on entering the dome, once per player per run (a tag), so a late
  joiner is kitted on arrival.
- **Late joiners** start on the shore and walk the open tunnels to catch up.
- **Automatons.** Zombies, skeletons and husks in copper or iron armour with
  names; no creepers, endermen or anything that damages blocks. Summoned
  with NBT so there are no babies, jockeys, reinforcements or item pickup.
  Removed when nobody is in their dome.
- **Reset** is a function per dome that puts back the blocks and entities
  that change, plus one that zeroes the scores and tags. The build script
  remains the way to rebuild from nothing.
- **One forceloaded area** only if the datapack needs it; functions run
  without loaded chunks, but block changes need them. Decide in stage 0 and
  record it.

## Provisional decisions (taken while the owner was away: confirm or change)

From the review of the six dome designs, 25 Nov 2026. Each is the
reviewer's recommendation and each is cheap to change back.

- **Leaving a dome** never costs more than the stage in progress: finished
  stages (a pump, a quarter of charge, a round, a shut main, laid pipe) are
  kept until the game's reset. So the Gearworks keeps its pipe when left;
  only the reset clears it (this replaces "Entering clears an old attempt").
- **A change between the solo and two-player forms** never shuts, removes
  or restarts something a player is using; it takes effect at the room's
  next safe point.
- **Gearworks steam rule:** a pipe is lit if it is within N lengths of the
  chest along laid pipe; extra pipe never steals steam.
- **Clockwork Maze:** the two-key Regulator and the held gates apply only
  while two players are actually there, so a late second player never
  strands the first.
- **Story:** the engineer T. Marsh, who wrote the Boiler Room's log, is the
  Master the boss fight ends with; the later logs are by Chief Engineer
  O. Finch. Logs are four pages at most.
- **Automatons:** Stoker (zombie, copper), Riveter (skeleton, bow), Slag
  Husk (husk, iron).
- **An unfinished dome** has a BYPASS lever by its exit, so the ring can
  always be played end to end.
- **The run** is trimmed towards an hour: the designs summed to about 65
  minutes; targets are Gearworks 7, Flooded Dome 8, Foundry 7, Signal Room
  5.5, Maze 7.5, the Engine 10.

## Stages

Each stage ends with: the offline checks, a build into the world, the verify
script, and (stages 1 to 5) a play-test by the owner. Do not start the next
stage until the owner has played the last one and said what to change.

**Stage 0: ground work (no play-test).**
- Survey the ocean: depth and floor over the site; choose the positions of
  the dock, the shore landing and all eight domes so tunnels are 20 to 40
  blocks long and the ring closes. Record the layout and render a plan.
- Prove the mechanisms in a rig before designing round them: a datapack
  loads and ticks in this server version (pack format, function syntax);
  a flooded glass tube can be drained section by section and refilled
  (it needs a barrier bulkhead where the dry part meets the flooded part,
  or the water runs back in);
  a dome can be built dry under water (make it solid glass, then hollow
  it: placing the shell and clearing the water row by row leaks while the
  game ticks); text displays for the timers; bossbars; a trophy sign with
  `can_place_on`; a boat on the shore that is replaced when taken.
- Decide how the datapack reaches the world's folder from the build, and
  how a rebuild replaces it.
- Read `examples/castle2` for patterns worth copying (stage list, `*_cells`
  shared by build and verify, record/replay against a fake client, how its
  checks work a mechanism over RCON, the limits it met). Copy patterns, do
  not import its modules.
- Write the skeleton: `station_layout.py`, `build_station.py` (stages),
  `datapack.py` (generator), `verify_station.py`, `README.md`,
  `PROGRESS.md`, and a unit test that runs the offline checks.

*State:* done.

**Stage 3:** Foundry and the Signal Room. **Owner plays.**

## Checks

- `verify_station.py` works every mechanism in place over RCON: each
  puzzle's success and failure paths in solo and two-player forms (stand-ins
  for players where a position or a tag is what the game reads), each tunnel
  draining and refilling, each battle's waves raised, killed and counted,
  the reset from a scrambled state, the vote's countdown expiring, the count
  falling back to solo.
- What RCON cannot stand in for goes in `PROGRESS.md` as a list for the
  owner's play-test (restricted items in a real hand, jumps, the feel and
  fairness of fights, sounds and titles).
- Never claim a thing works unless a check exercised it or the owner played it.

## Not in the plan

- An automated player (a bot client or fake players). Raised and set aside:
  the owner play-tests each stage.
- Per-player scores, a lives counter, more than two players needed for
  anything.
